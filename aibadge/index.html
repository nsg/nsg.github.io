Self-reported repository disclosure

How I use AI in a project.

These badges are my shorthand for showing how generative AI contributed to one of my repositories—and how closely I reviewed and understand the result.

What the badge describes

My process for the current committed implementation: AI contribution, human review, and how well I understand the code.

What it does not certify

Quality, security, correctness, ownership, or fitness for use. The label is disclosure—not a score or seal of approval.

Choose the closest description.

The wording defines each level; color only helps distinguish them. Paste one badge near the top of a README and revisit it when the project changes.

My take

Disclosure beats guesswork.

I use these badges to say something concrete about my process. A badge is not a verdict on the software: human-written code can be poor, and AI-written code can appear to work. The useful distinction is how the work was produced and how much human understanding stands behind it.

The scale considers committed implementation—not prompt count, a particular model, dependencies, or incidental AI research. It is intentionally self-reported and personal, not a universal standard or independent certification.

The definitions use “I” because they describe my repositories. A team adopting the idea should interpret the label across the contributors and use “we” in its own explanation.
